READ-ONLY DESIGN REVIEW / 2 OCTOBER 2026 / COGVEST 1.0.18

Clearer numbers.
Uneven visual hierarchy.

CogVest now has a coherent daily-use interface. The holdings weight bars, shorter asset names and separate P&L metrics work well. It still spends too much space on controls and context before showing the information people came to see.

This is a refinement problem, not a reason to rewrite the app. The strongest direction is to extend the compact holdings approach: clear alignment, honest visual quantities, short labels, and useful detail on demand. More cards, gradients or decorative illustrations would not solve the observed problems.

Prioritized findings

No blocking visual failure was observed in the settled, normal-text screens. P2 means a usability or interpretation issue worth fixing; P3 means polish or a lower-confidence design concern. These are expert-review findings, not measured user-study results.

P2 / DATA VISUALIZATION / SMALL CHANGE, CAREFUL VERIFICATION

1. Smoothing suggests movement that monthly records do not establish

The Minimal Progress line visibly dips between April and May even though the monthly history increases from 19.29L to 19.87L. The chart renders both portfolio and asset series with curved in src/features/progress/ProgressScreen.tsx:649,683. Rounded interpolation introduces apparent intra-month behavior into a month-end series.

Use straight segments with monthly points. Keep gaps, estimate disclosure and series distinctions. Verify that monotonically increasing stored values never draw a dip. This is more important than cosmetic chart changes.

Evidence: Minimal Progress, monthly records.

P2 / INTERACTION / SMALL

2. Expandable controls look like headings

Minimal Dashboard uses a centered, bold Invested & returns label; Progress uses Comparisons & breakdowns. Neither has the familiar chevron visible on Settings disclosures. The contents are correctly hidden, but the means of revealing them is visually weak. Add Holding's muted manual-entry and Use actions have a similar problem.

Use one compact disclosure pattern with a visible expand/collapse indicator and an explicit expanded state. Keep Minimal Mode genuinely quiet; do not restore all the hidden metrics by default.

Evidence: Dashboard, Progress, Add Holding.

P2 / HIERARCHY / MEDIUM

3. Page controls consume too much of the useful viewport

Holdings has a large title and icon wells, search, scope text, actions, then a generous gap before the first position. The first row starts roughly a third of the way down the normal-text screen. At 130% text size the action row wraps and pushes it further down. Dashboard separates the main value, no-activity message and allocation with substantial gaps.

Reduce inter-section spacing and the visual weight of icon backgrounds before shrinking text or touch targets. Consolidate list tools where they fit. Present an inactive month's activity as a compact status rather than a full section. Preserve separate Holding P&L and Holding P&L % columns and the proportional weight bars.

Evidence: Standard Holdings, large text, Dashboard.

P2 / PROGRESS / MEDIUM

4. Progress has too many introductions before its chart

The screen title, stored-values subtitle, month-end label, month selector, total, estimated-value label, estimate notice, chart title, chart scope and range controls form a long vertical sequence. The selected-month navigation sits below the chart, beyond the initial viewport. Standard Mode adds more financial metrics to this sequence.

Group the selected period with its value and status, shorten duplicate context, and place chart selection feedback beside the chart. Do not remove estimate warnings or conflate market change, contributions and return. The goal is fewer repeated introductions, not fewer financial safeguards.

Evidence: Standard Progress, Minimal Progress.

P2 / INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE / SMALL TO MEDIUM

5. Futures looks like part of backup administration

Settings places Binance USDT futures after Back up portfolio and Restore backup, before App information. There is no visible investing-tools heading separating it from the backup group. This makes a portfolio feature look like an administrative option.

Give investment tools a distinct group and review the existing contextual entry points. Do not add another permanent navigation tab without testing whether one is needed. App information and About can also be combined into a clearer administrative group.

Evidence: Settings lower section.

P3 / VISUAL CONSISTENCY / MEDIUM

6. Forms and daily screens do not yet share a consistent density

Daily screens use open sections and thin rules. Add Holding encloses its search and choices in a large surface. PPF uses long, generously spaced field groups. Futures starts with operational terminology and exact time controls. These are individually usable, but their type weight and spacing do not feel like one carefully tuned family.

Standardize section spacing, field labels and secondary actions. Reserve enclosing panels for meaningful decisions, warnings and review summaries. Keep instrument type, exchange and currency where they prevent a wrong selection. Do not flatten the backup warning or remove exact-time and FX entry from futures.

Evidence: Add Holding, PPF setup, Futures setup, holding detail.

P3 / MINIMAL MODE / SMALL

7. Some explanatory text survives after the related metrics disappear

Minimal Holdings hides returns, but its footer still says Returns are since investment, not today. Minimal Dashboard retains a sizable month-end snapshot explanation and the same large gaps between sections. Minimal is visibly different now, but it still carries some Standard Mode context.

Show the returns scope where returns are visible. Keep the current-versus-month-end distinction available, but make routine navigation to Progress shorter. Do not hide stale-price or incomplete-data warnings.

Evidence: Minimal Holdings, Minimal Dashboard.

P3 / CHART ENCODING / SMALL, VALIDATE WITH USERS

8. Monthly History's bars do not identify what length represents

The table places a full-width gray bar below each month, alongside Portfolio and Vs invested columns. It is not visually explicit which quantity controls the bar. Positive and negative return rows both have positive-length bars. This is an interpretation risk, not evidence of a calculation error.

Associate each bar with its portfolio-value cell, or provide one concise scale label. Preserve the user's chosen Vs invested metric and its cash-exclusion scope. Do not reintroduce repetitive per-row comparison sentences.

Evidence: Monthly History.

Look and feel

The black canvas, restrained palette, tabular money values and flat sections are consistent. The app no longer needs another visual concept. What keeps it from feeling fully polished is that titles, icon wells, field containers and section gaps often carry more visual weight than their importance warrants.

The holding-weight line is the most useful expression of character in the current design: it makes data easier to read. Carry that principle forward through precise chart geometry, aligned value pairs, consistent disclosure rows and tighter list controls. Avoid adding a logo to every screen, a new metaphor, decorative gradients or more colored panels.

Typography needs a clearer role hierarchy rather than a font replacement. Page titles can be quieter; primary financial values should stay prominent; labels should remain legible. Secondary actions should look interactive consistently. Preserve signed gain/loss values so color is not the only cue.

Recommended work order

  1. Fix chart interpolation and add visual/data checks for monotonic monthly series.
  2. Unify disclosure affordances and clean up Minimal Mode's context-only text.
  3. Tighten the shared header, tools and section-spacing system. Check normal and large text before applying it broadly.
  4. Refine Progress's period/value/status grouping and Monthly History's bar encoding.
  5. Regroup Settings and bring forms into the same spacing and action hierarchy.

Start with a before/after comparison of Holdings and Progress. Reuse shared components, keep the existing routes and financial model, and verify each batch on an installed build. Do not trade legibility or touch-target size for density.

Method and limits

This review uses fresh screenshots captured during this audit from an installed local development APK, version 1.0.18/code 19, serving the merged PR511 source. It is not a claim that the newly generated cloud preview APK was tested.

Environment: CogVest_UX_Proof, Android API 36, 1280 by 2856 pixels, density 480, normal text and a focused 130% Holdings check. Reduced motion was enabled. The synthetic fixture has four positions and is heavily concentrated in Bitcoin. No real financial documents were opened, no records were saved, and no import/export or restore was executed. Standard Mode and font scale 1.0 were restored. Metro and the audit's ADB reverse connection were stopped.

Navigation timing initially produced wrong-screen and transitional captures, which were rejected and recaptured. The first Maestro capture flow stopped when Settings retained a lower scroll position; a follow-up scrolled to the mode control and restored Standard Mode successfully. Text-size changes also caused a transient clipping state; the accepted large-text evidence is after a cold restart. This does not establish a production regression or a clean bill of health for live font-size changes.

Not covered: all error/loading/empty variants, populated futures and PPF workflows, imported mutual-fund holdings, long portfolios, keyboard behavior, landscape/tablets, TalkBack, measured contrast compliance, animation quality, and fresh-install onboarding. No financial correctness or performance claims are made. Portfolio concentration and legacy fixture memo text are not product-design defects.

Recommendations about spacing, alignment and meaningful grouping are consistent with Android's layout guidance. That guidance also emphasizes flexible layouts, safe areas and reachable interactions. It supports the review criteria; the screenshots below support the app-specific findings.
